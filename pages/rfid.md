# RFID Labels & RFID Tags

Source: [https://www.labtag.com/solutions/rfid-labels-tags/](https://www.labtag.com/solutions/rfid-labels-tags/)

![RFID NitroTAG cryogenic labels with printer and reader](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/RF3-JTTA-9-use2-1.jpg "/media/rfid-labels-tags/RF3-JTTA-9-use2-1.jpg")

Radio-frequency identification (RFID) is a powerful tool used by hospitals, histopathology labs, research facilities, and IVF labs for record keeping, access control, inventory tracking, sample and patient management, and much more. Our RFID solutions are outfitted with flexible NFC, HF, and UHF inlays that allow them to be applied to various surfaces or our durable, resistant labels—creating RFID labels suited to harsh environments.

- **Xylene & Chemical-Resistant Labels** — Histopathology slide and cassette tracking
- **RFID Tags** — Flexible and non-adhesive RFID tags for specialty surfaces
- **RFID Printers** — Thermal-transfer printers that print and encode RFID labels

### By Benefit

- **Printed & Encoded** — Serialized barcodes plus encrypted RFID data
- **Flexible Inlays** — Passive, battery-free inlays for curved surfaces

### By Solution

- **Custom RFID** — Serialized barcodes, logos, TID/UID encoding, and verification
- **Industrial RFID** — Extra-durable RFID labels and tags for harsh industrial environments

## RFID Products

Cryogenic RFID vial labels support record keeping, inventory tracking, and sample management in hospitals, research facilities, and IVF labs. Flexible passive inlays conform to curved vial surfaces, and thermal-transfer printing adds serialized text and barcodes alongside encoded RFID data through liquid nitrogen and ultra-low storage.

### Autoclave-Resistant RFID Labels

![Autoclave-resistant RFID labels](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/RF3-AUTT-28-use6-1.jpg "/media/rfid-labels-tags/RF3-AUTT-28-use6-1.jpg")

Autoclave-resistant RFID labels create durable identification for harsh sterilization environments—supporting inventory tracking and sample management through steam sterilization and related workflows without losing adhesive performance or RFID readability.

### Deep-Freeze RFID Microplate Labels

![Deep-freeze RFID microplate labels with handheld reader](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/RFP-550-use3.jpg "/media/rfid-labels-tags/RFP-550-use3.jpg")

Deep-freeze RFID microplate labels support batch scanning without direct line of sight. RFID smart labels can be read simultaneously from a few inches to a few feet—even when covered or embedded in a container—improving inventory accuracy and workflow monitoring in freezer-based lab processes.

### Xylene & Chemical-Resistant Labels

![Xylene-resistant RFID labels for microscope slides](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/RF3-FTT-98-use5.jpg "/media/rfid-labels-tags/RF3-FTT-98-use5.jpg")

Xylene and chemical-resistant RFID labels are suited to histopathology labs for record keeping and sample tracking. Durable facestocks withstand chemical exposure and a wide temperature range so slide and cassette IDs stay readable through staining and related protocols.

### RFID Tags

![Flexible PingTAG RFID tags with handheld reader](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/RF3-VPT-73BM-use3-2.jpg "/media/rfid-labels-tags/RF3-VPT-73BM-use3-2.jpg")

Flexible and non-adhesive RFID tags extend identification beyond printable labels—ideal when adhesive constructs are not the right fit. Pair tags with handheld readers for omnidirectional scanning across racks, boxes, and specialty surfaces in research and clinical workflows.

### RFID Printers

![Zebra RFID printer for thermal-transfer RFID labels](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/ZD621R-800x600px.jpg "/media/rfid-labels-tags/ZD621R-800x600px.jpg")

Thermal RFID labels are compatible with most thermal-transfer RFID printers, allowing serialized text and barcodes to print while the inlay is encoded. Combining barcodes and RFID integrates tags and labels into laboratory management systems—speeding sample gatekeeping, inventorying, and tracking.

### RFID Readers

![Industrial RFID reader kit](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/EARG630-KIT-thumb5.jpg "/media/rfid-labels-tags/EARG630-KIT-thumb5.jpg")

Passive RFID labels and tags need an integrator system to store and process encoded information. Handheld readers support omnidirectional scanning without full visibility or direct line of sight—ideal for racks, boxes, and batch reads across the lab.

## Benefits of RFID Labels & RFID Tags

### Wide Scanning Radius

![RFID batch scanning with handheld reader](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/RFP-550-use3.jpg "/media/rfid-labels-tags/RFP-550-use3.jpg")

RFID smart labels can be scanned and read in batches, simultaneously, from a few inches to a few feet in range and without full visibility or direct line of sight. RFID tags emit their signal within a wide omnidirectional read range, even when covered or embedded within a container—giving greater freedom in tag placement and reducing the risk of labels falling off or being removed.

### Printed & Encoded

![Thermal-transfer RFID printer encoding labels](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/ZD621R-800x600px.jpg "/media/rfid-labels-tags/ZD621R-800x600px.jpg")

RFID labels and tags can be encoded with far more information than barcodes, and the technology protects your data with encryption and physical shielding. Thermal RFID labels print serialized text and barcodes on compatible printers so barcodes and RFID fully integrate into laboratory management systems for gatekeeping, inventorying, and tracking.

### Flexible Inlays

![Flexible RFID inlays on specialty tags](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/RF3-VPT-73BM-use3-2.jpg "/media/rfid-labels-tags/RF3-VPT-73BM-use3-2.jpg")

Our RFID smart labels use a passive inlay that does not require a battery. That permits much smaller sizes, while the inlay’s flexible nature allows application to curved and varied surfaces. Passive RFID labels and tags still require an integrator system to store and process the encoded information.

### Applications

![Cryogenic RFID labels in laboratory use](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/RF3-JTTA-9-use2-1.jpg "/media/rfid-labels-tags/RF3-JTTA-9-use2-1.jpg")

The primary benefit of RFID labeling for healthcare is the reduction of misidentification errors. In clinics and research facilities, RFID tags allow patient samples to be tracked in real time from collection to testing. They also improve supply-chain accuracy, can be programmed to send medical alerts, and help locate valuable samples—making workflow monitoring easier with RFID thermal labels.

## RFID Solutions

### Custom RFID

![Custom color RFID labels](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/RFID-Custom-Color-Labels-3.jpg "/media/rfid-labels-tags/RFID-Custom-Color-Labels-3.jpg")

Beyond printable RFID labels and tags, solutions can be custom printed with serialized barcodes, color logos, and encoded alphanumeric values. TID and UID reading and printing are available on RFID labels and flexible/non-adhesive tags, with verification that the unique RFID code matches the printed data. Factory-locked TID/UID values cannot change after production—simplifying tamper detection for anti-counterfeiting. From inlay, antenna, chip, and size to configuration and material, GA International builds specialty RFID labels and tags for dependable tracking.

### Industrial RFID

![Industrial-grade RFID labeling kit](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/EARG630-KIT-thumb5.jpg "/media/rfid-labels-tags/EARG630-KIT-thumb5.jpg")

Industrial-grade RFID solutions deliver extra-durable labels and tags designed for specific industries and the harshest working environments—extending LabTAG RFID identification beyond the lab into manufacturing and field conditions that demand rugged constructs.
