# ResiTAG®

Source: [https://www.labtag.com/solutions/resitag-2/](https://www.labtag.com/solutions/resitag-2/)

![ResiTAG labels for resin-embedding capsules](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/XST-251-use2.jpg "/media/resitag-2/XST-251-use2.jpg")



ResiTAG® is an identification solution for samples used in electron microscopy. While imaging technologies have advanced, sample identification often remains handwritten on paper embedded in resin blocks or limited to the storage container. That practice can lead to mix-ups, so ResiTAG® provides secure, permanent identification for resin blocks.

These resin- and baking-resistant labels are available alone or in a kit with a thermal-transfer printer, barcode printing software, and matching ribbon for resin-embedded samples.

## ResiTAG® Guide

Everything you need to know before choosing ResiTAG®. Browse options by capability below.

### By Capability

- **Chemical Resistance** — Withstands embedding in resin used in microscopy
- **Heat Resistance** — Withstands resin-baking temperatures up to +150°C
- **Printable IDs** — Print many lines of text and barcodes on a tiny label
- **Capsule & Mold Fit** — Identify samples in BEEM® capsules, gelatin capsules, and molds
- **Thermal-Transfer** — Compatible with all major brands of thermal-transfer printers

## Why Use ResiTAG®

### Clear, Legible Sample IDs

![ResiTAG thermal-transfer labels and printer](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/XST-251-use.png "/media/resitag-2/XST-251-use.png")

With ResiTAG® you can print many lines of text on a tiny label—far more than handwriting allows. Print hundreds of labels at once for clear, legible identifiers. The chemical-resistant printout will not smear in liquid or baked resin. You can scan barcodes into Excel, reducing the possibility of human error.

## How to Use ResiTAG®

### Print, Insert, Bake, Track

ResiTAG® labels are easy to use. Print the labels on a thermal-transfer printer with text and barcodes, then insert the label into the capsule or mold containing your sample and liquid resin. Bake your samples in the histological oven. You now have a unique identifier for each sample that you can track in a spreadsheet or database.
