# Cryogenic Labels

Source: [https://www.labtag.com/solutions/cryogenic-labels/](https://www.labtag.com/solutions/cryogenic-labels/)

![Cryogenic Labels](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/JTTA-29-use2-Copie-e1617026541770.jpg "/media/cryogenic-labels/JTTA-29-use2-Copie-e1617026541770.jpg")

An ID that goes in the Dewar has to survive liquid nitrogen, vapor phase, and the scanner when the rack comes back up.

LabTAG cryogenic labels are specified for LN₂ (−196°C) from the first stick. Apply at ambient—or to a wet tube—then immerse. Deep-freeze stock and already-frozen surfaces are different constructs: keep those guides if the vial never sees a Dewar, or if it is already frozen when you label.

## The tank is not a freezer

![Cryogenic labels on vials and tubes](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/JTTA-103-Use-vials.png "/media/cryogenic-labels/JTTA-103-Use-vials.png")

Office stock and “ultra-low” faces lift, crack, or fog when they hit LN₂. Cryogenic adhesive is specified for liquid and vapor-phase nitrogen, then for the freeze–thaw that happens every time a box leaves the Dewar. There is no cure time: label, rack, immerse.

Permanent adhesive takes the full storage cycle. Removable peels clean when the ID is temporary. Either way, spec for the coldest step the vial will see—not the temperature on the bench when you print. Deep-freeze stock belongs on its own page if the vial never sees a Dewar. Already-frozen surfaces belong on CryoSTUCK®.

> Specify the construct for the tank, not the room.

## Tubes, vials, boxes, and caps

![Laser-printable cryogenic vials](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/CL-23T1-Use.png "/media/cryogenic-labels/CL-23T1-Use.png")

The die follows the container. Cryo vials and Eppendorf-style microtubes take a wrap or a side rectangle plus a cap dot. Cardboard and polypropylene freezer boxes take a larger face that still reads after frost.

- Cryo vials and microtubes — including Eppendorf-style tubes
- Freezer boxes — cardboard and polypropylene
- Caps and color dots — tube and vial caps, cryo marker
- Wet-surface stick — no wait before the tank

## Plates, PCR, and tags

![Labeled PCR tubes in a thermocycler](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/FJT-269-Use-2.jpg "/media/pcr-labels-and-tags/FJT-269-Use-2.jpg")

Plates are a flat problem. Waterproof thermoplastic stays flat off the liner so it lands on cell-culture, microwell, 96- and 384-well PCR plates, and Petri dishes—including automated labelers.

PCR is a thermocycler problem. Faces mark the side or cap of 0.2 ml tubes and 8-well strips and still go through the cycle. PCR-TagTrax™ is the non-adhesive tag when a wrap is the wrong geometry.

- Cell-culture plates — microwell and PCR plates
- Petri dishes — stay flat off the liner
- PCR tubes — high-profile 0.2 ml and 8-well strips
- PCR-TagTrax™ — non-adhesive tags when a wrap is the wrong geometry

## Metal racks, canes, and IVF straws

![Cryogenic labels for metal racks and canes](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/AWA-28-Use-1.png "/media/cryogenic-labels/AWA-28-Use-1.png")

Metal racks, aluminum cassettes, stainless-steel boxes, sleeves, and canes need a face that bonds to metal, not only polypropylene. The same tank that is kind to a wrapped vial will lift a paper label off a cane. Cryo-StrawTAG™ is the low-VOC IVF straw label for 0.25 ml and 0.5 ml straws, including high-security vitrification devices.

- Metal racks — canes, sleeves, and stainless-steel boxes
- Aluminum cassettes — film cans and tools that go in the Dewar
- Cryo-StrawTAG™ — 0.25 ml and 0.5 ml IVF straws
- Vitrification — Cryotop® and other high-security devices

## Print on the printer you already run

Thermal-transfer is the barcode workhorse for small-diameter tubes and boxes. Direct thermal skips the ribbon. Laser and inkjet run from letter or A4 sheets when the only printer in the room is an office machine. DYMO 450-series cryo stock immerses in LN₂ right after print. Color dots take a smudge-proof cryo marker. Automation faces are cut for print-and-apply.

LabelWriter™ 5-Series frozen-surface stock belongs on the CryoSTUCK® page, not here.

- Thermal-transfer — barcodes for tubes, vials, and boxes
- Direct thermal — when you do not want a ribbon
- Laser and inkjet — sheets or rolls, including full-color logos
- DYMO 450-series — LabelWriter™ cryo stock
- Writable dots — thermoplastic caps and tube sides
- Print-and-apply — automation labels for vials, tubes, and plates

## When one peel has to do two jobs

![SnapPEEL cryogenic dual labels](https://cdn.labtag.com/wp-content/uploads/JTTA-560-Use4.jpg "/media/cryogenic-labels/JTTA-560-Use4.jpg")

A cryo ID is often two IDs. SnapPEEL™ peels a cap dot and a side rectangle in one motion so gloves do not lose the small piece. Wrap-around laminate protects print through abrasion and solvents. SimPEEL™ is the adhesive-free tab so the face does not lift with the glove.

RFID UHF inlays scan in LN₂ without line of sight, with 1D/2D still on the face. Blackout cover-ups for vials that are already frozen live with CryoSTUCK®—do not spec them from this page.

- SnapPEEL™ — connected circle-and-rectangle dual IDs
- Wrap-around — self-laminating protection for microtubes and cryo vials
- SimPEEL™ — adhesive-free tab for gloved peels
- Tamper-evident — stock that tears on removal
- Piggyback — nests a second label until the aliquot needs its own ID
- Transparent faces — keep the contents visible
- UHF RFID — scannable in LN₂ without line of sight
